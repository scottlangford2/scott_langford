Readings

Weekly Readings

Free, publicly available readings that show each week’s ideas at work in real public administration and Texas politics — two to three per week. All free, links only; no login required.

Module 1 — Research Questions & Excel Foundations

Week 1 — Analytical questions & why evidence matters

  • The Next Era of Data-driven Local Government — Route Fifty — https://www.route-fifty.com/digital-government/2022/11/next-era-data-driven-local-government/380156/ — How cities use data to set budgets, spot inequities, and rethink services (via Bloomberg’s What Works Cities) — why analytics matter in local government.
  • Evidence-Based Policymaking Primer — Bipartisan Policy Center — https://bipartisanpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Evidence-Based-Policymaking-Primer.pdf — Short, nonpartisan take on how governments use (and misuse) evidence.

Week 2 — Working with real data in Excel

  • ⭐ The Reinhart-Rogoff error — or how not to Excel at economics — The Conversation — https://theconversation.com/the-reinhart-rogoff-error-or-how-not-to-excel-at-economics-13646 — A single Excel range error helped justify global austerity — why careful spreadsheet work matters.
  • Data Organization in Spreadsheets — Broman & Woo — https://kbroman.org/dataorg/ — Practical rules for structuring data in a spreadsheet; reinforces the cell/table foundations in Lectures 3–4.

Module 2 — Distributions & Descriptive Statistics

Week 3 — Frequency tables, histograms, charts, reading distributions

  • Texas voter turnout dropped in the 2024 election [TX] — The Texas Tribune — https://www.texastribune.org/2024/11/06/texas-voter-turnout-election-2024-registration/ — Interactive maps/charts of turnout across all 254 counties — read a real distribution as map, ranking, and histogram.
  • How to Spot Visualization Lies — Nathan Yau, FlowingData — https://flowingdata.com/2017/02/09/how-to-spot-visualization-lies/ — A data-viz expert’s quick guide to truncated axes, dual axes, and other chart tricks — the “misleading vs. honest charts” skill.

Week 4 — Center, spread, skew & outliers

  • Housing affordability gap hits Texas [TX] — Texas Comptroller, Fiscal Notes — https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/fiscal-notes/economics/2024/aff-housing/ — A state analysis built entirely on median home prices and incomes — why analysts use the median for skewed data.
  • Median Household Income Increased in 2023… — U.S. Census Bureau — https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/income-poverty-health-insurance-coverage.html — The nation’s statistics agency reports income as a median, not a mean — an authoritative reason the distinction matters.

Module 3 — Normal Curve & Probability

Week 5 — The normal curve, z-scores, standardizing

  • Texas students’ STAAR scores are out — see how your district did [TX] — The Texas Tribune — https://www.texastribune.org/2025/08/16/texas-staar-scores-lookup/ — Raw scores reported as scale scores and statewide comparisons — the “convert a raw score to a standardized/percentile score” move.
  • STAAR 100-Point Scale Tables [TX] — Texas Education Agency — https://tea.texas.gov/data-reports/student-assessment-results/staar-100-point-scale-tables — TEA’s official explanation that the scale is defined by percentiles — a real standardization example using the state test.

Week 6 — Probability rules & applied probability in government

  • Probability of Precipitation — National Weather Service (NOAA) — https://www.weather.gov/lmk/pops — A government agency explains what “30% chance of rain” actually means, and how the public misreads it.
  • The Normal Distribution in Excel — Real Statistics Using Excel — https://real-statistics.com/normal-distribution/ — Using the normal curve to find the proportion of cases above or below a value, computed in Excel (NORM.DIST).

Module 4 — Sampling & Hypothesis Testing

Week 7 — Sampling distributions, CLT, confidence intervals

  • Latest University of Texas / Texas Politics Project Poll [TX] — UT-Austin — https://texaspolitics.utexas.edu/latest-poll — A live Texas poll (~1,200 voters, margin ≈ ±2.83) — see sample size → margin of error in a real political context.
  • Understanding the margin of error in election polls — Pew Research Center — https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2016/09/08/understanding-the-margin-of-error-in-election-polls/ — Sample size → margin of error and the 95% level; the margin on a lead is roughly double.

Week 8 — Hypothesis-testing logic, p-values, significance

  • The ASA Statement on p-Values — American Statistical Association — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00031305.2016.1154108 — The authoritative six principles: a p-value is NOT the probability the null is true, and NOT an effect size.
  • Scientific method: Statistical errors — Regina Nuzzo, Nature (2014) — https://www.nature.com/articles/506150a — The famous, readable feature on why p-values are “surprisingly slippery” and significance ≠ importance.

Week 9 — One-sample t-test (mean vs. a benchmark)

  • One-Sample t-Test in Excel — Real Statistics Using Excel — https://real-statistics.com/students-t-distribution/one-sample-t-test/ — Frames the test as “is the mean equal to a claimed value?” and works it in Excel — matches the course tooling.
  • Talarico leads Paxton by 5 points in new Texas Senate poll [TX] — The Texas Tribune — https://www.texastribune.org/2026/07/28/texas-senate-poll-james-talarico-ken-paxton-july-2026/ — A current Texas poll as a benchmark hook: is a 5-point lead distinguishable from zero given the margin?

Module 5 — t-Tests, Part I

Week 10 — Independent-samples t-test (two separate groups)

  • Texas school-district higher-ed outcomes [TX] — The Texas Tribune — https://www.texastribune.org/2025/12/18/texas-school-district-higher-education-outcomes-lookup-data/ — A clean two-group gap: 16% of economically disadvantaged students earn a credential vs. 36% of others.
  • Reducing No-shows for Passport Appointments — U.S. Office of Evaluation Sciences (GSA) — https://oes.gsa.gov/projects/2313-reducing-no-shows-passport-appts/ — A real federal A/B test (treatment vs. control) where the difference was NOT significant — honest group comparison.

Module 6 — t-Tests, Part II

Week 11 — Paired-samples t-test (before/after, matched)

  • Left-Turn Calming Pilot Evaluation [TX] — City of Austin, Vision Zero — https://www.austintexas.gov/sites/default/files/files/TPW/VisionZero/VZ-Analytics-Left-Turn-Calming-Pilot-Evaluation-English.pdf — The same 16 intersections measured before vs. after — crashes down 46%. Textbook paired design, in a Texas city.
  • Paired-Samples t-Test in Excel — Real Statistics Using Excel — https://real-statistics.com/students-t-distribution/paired-sample-t-test/ — The before/after (dependent) t-test worked step-by-step in Excel — the design behind the Vision Zero pilot.

Week 12 — Choosing the right test; statistical vs. practical significance

  • Decreasing SNAP Denials Due to Incomplete Information — U.S. Office of Evaluation Sciences (GSA) — https://oes.gsa.gov/projects/2310-decreasing-snap-denial-rates/ — A redesign cut denials 1.1 points but didn’t change enrollment — statistical detectability vs. whether it matters.
  • The Ultimate Guide to t-Tests — GraphPad — https://www.graphpad.com/guides/the-ultimate-guide-to-t-tests — Lays out one-sample vs. paired vs. independent and when to use each — scaffolding for the “choose the right test” exercise.

Module 7 — Regression

Week 13 — Correlation, scatterplots, simple regression; correlation ≠ causation

  • Spurious Correlations — Tyler Vigen — https://www.tylervigen.com/spurious-correlations — The canonical, funny illustration that variables can track together for no causal reason.
  • Texas voter turnout fell from 2018 — but topped the last midterm [TX] — The Texas Tribune — https://www.texastribune.org/2022/11/10/texas-voter-turnout-2022/ — Turnout co-varies with county type — a live correlation-vs-causation discussion on the course’s county panel.

Week 14 — Multiple regression; coefficients; model fit

  • Multiple Regression Analysis in Excel — Real Statistics Using Excel — https://real-statistics.com/multiple-regression/multiple-regression-analysis/ — Interpreting coefficients, R², and controlling for other predictors — worked in Excel, the course tool.
  • The Ultimate Guide to Linear Regression — GraphPad — https://www.graphpad.com/guides/the-ultimate-guide-to-linear-regression — Interpreting slope, intercept, R², and model fit — a companion to the multiple-regression walkthrough.

Module 8 — Final Project & Communicating Findings

Week 15 — Doing a full analysis (data prep → results)

  • The Opportunity Gap: About the Data and Our Analysis — ProPublica — https://www.propublica.org/article/opportunity-gap-methodology — End-to-end model: public data → cleaning/outlier checks → regression → public interactive.
  • Measuring the Texas economy with high-frequency data [TX] — Texas Comptroller, Fiscal Notes — https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/fiscal-notes/archive/2022/feb/data.php — Turning a question into an analysis by choosing indicators — and being candid about data limits.

Week 16 — Communicating findings; honest data viz

  • 10 Ways to Mislead with Data Visualization — Jon Schwabish, PolicyViz — https://policyviz.com/2023/02/07/10-ways-to-mislead-with-data-visualization/ — A concrete catalog of tricks (dual axes, truncated bars, 3D pies) from a public-sector data-viz expert.
  • How to Lie with Charts — Hands-On Data Visualization (open textbook) — https://handsondataviz.org/how-to-lie-with-charts.html — Free chapter demonstrating how axes/aspect ratio exaggerate change — paired with the ethic of honest defaults.
