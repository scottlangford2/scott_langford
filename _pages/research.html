I study how local governments and community organizations absorb shocks. Extreme weather, financial crises, and the opioid epidemic are the shocks; city and county governments, nonprofits, community banks, and small employers are the organizations. Across the three lines of work below the questions are the same administrative ones — which organizations fail and which survive, what the survivors cut in order to stay open, and whether the public programs meant to buffer these events actually do. I answer them with applied econometrics on administrative data I build from public records that were never designed to be read at scale.

Currently working on

  • How extreme weather moves the balance sheets of city and county governments, using annual financial reports
  • What local governments talk about when they talk about data — measured across two million meeting records
  • Which nonprofits survive a disaster, and what the survivors give up to do it
  • Cost-benefit analysis for the Town of Clayton, NC (Lookout Analytics)
