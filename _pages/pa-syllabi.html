⬇ Download the course data (CSV)📋 Method & limits✉ Contribute a syllabus

Individual syllabi are not published here. No syllabus is hosted, linked or listed, and no instructor is named. Every entry below describes a course, pooled from all the syllabi held for it.
A course is only described once enough people have contributed one. It needs at least 3 syllabi from at least 2 different institutions. Counting syllabi alone would not be enough — three syllabi for one course from one department are plausibly the same instructor across three terms, and describing them in public would describe that person's syllabus with the filename removed. Requiring separate institutions is what prevents that. Everything below that line is withheld entirely: 35 courses covering 54 syllabi are omitted from this report.

What happens to it. It is read once to extract topics, assigned works, software and assessment types, and those facts join the course description above. The document goes to private storage with no public address; it is never published, never linked, never listed and never shared. You are not named, and no instructor name is recorded from a contribution. You get a withdrawal link that deletes the file outright, not just a flag.

Method & limits

The frame is every institution awarding a degree in public administration or public policy (IPEDS CIP 44.04/44.05): 1,013 programs at 537 institutions across all 50 states. Syllabi are gathered only from institutions' own public websites and portals — several publish them under a state mandate — plus openly licensed courseware. Commercial platforms are never retrieved from. Harvesting obeys robots.txt and identifies itself.

Courses below the threshold are not shown at all — not shown with small numbers, not shown anonymised. They are counted in the omitted total and otherwise absent, and a contribution is what moves a course across that line.

This is a sample, and a biased one. Only a minority of syllabi are public at all, and the states that require posting are heavily over-represented. Read these as patterns among the syllabi that happen to be public, not as what the field as a whole teaches. Descriptions are assembled from extracted fields — topics, assigned works, assessment types — not paraphrased from the documents, so every clause traces to a column you can check in the CSV.

A local model screens the citation lines. A syllabus's deadlines, section numbers and file names look like citations to a pattern matcher, and they were being published as assigned texts. A model running on the same machine judges each candidate line; it can only reject a line the document contains, never write one, and a line it is unsure about is kept. No description is generated by a model.
