excerpt: 'Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) Hazard Mitigation Assistance (HMA) grants have been recognized as an effective way to reduce natural disaster losses. However, most mitigation studies assess the cost-effectiveness of such funding based on projected losses. This study empirically assesses the extent to which grant funding reduced counties'' natural hazard induced deaths, injuries, and damages from 2005-2020 using the Spatial Hazard Events and Losses database and FEMA''s Hazard Mitigation Assistance Projects database. Results from a distributed lag model indicate that HMA grants reduced disaster losses in excess of their cost over a 15 year period.'
